How blips work

The idea you had in July, back in front of you in September.

A blip is a thought you are not ready to do anything with yet. Casey keeps hold of it and puts a few on your Today page each morning, picked at random from everything you have parked.

The day you think of it

You type one line and get on with your morning. There is no folder to choose and nothing to organise afterwards.

A few weeks later

It turns up beside that day's writing. Sometimes you act on it. Most days you read it again and leave it where it is.

The fifth time round

The tally shows how often an idea has come back. When the same one keeps returning, that is usually worth paying attention to.

The ideas you have seen least often come back soonest, so nothing sits at the bottom of a list for a year going stale.

What you are signing up for

Free, private, and yours to take away.

It costs nothing

An account is free. There are no adverts anywhere in Casey, and nothing is sold on the side.

Nothing follows you around

No analytics and no tracking scripts. Signing up takes a username and a password, and you can turn on two-factor login if you want it.

Your writing stays yours

Export the lot as JSON or CSV whenever you like. If you would rather keep it on your own machine, Casey runs in Docker and the source is public.

Casey exists to get out of your way, so that you can be present. Add what you need, ignore what you don't.

Inspired by Casey Newton's productivity system. Use it daily or weekly. There are no streaks to maintain, no points to earn, and no guilt if you skip a day.
